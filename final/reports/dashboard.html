Cell size grows from 5.0 cm near the sensor to 50.0 cm at 100.0 m (a 10.0x resolution falloff), keeping safety-critical detail close by while the far field stays cheap. Every panel is compared against a uniform 5 cm baseline over the same sensor footprint.

Drivability layer - 3 walkable-ground tiers

Walkable ground is split into firm (33,034 cells, 93.5% of walkable ground), moderate (1,468 cells, 4.2% of walkable ground), rough (835 cells, 2.4% of walkable ground) by counting how many of the slope and roughness criteria each cell violates. Mean slope on walkable ground is 0.153 against a 0.35 threshold, and mean roughness is 0.0114 m against a 0.08 m threshold. Static obstacles (746 cells) are drawn in the obstacle colour instead of a tier colour.

Sampling density - the foveation made visible

Colour is the sampling density in cells per m2 on a log ramp, so the drop from the 5.0 cm inner band to the 50.0 cm outer band reads as one continuous gradient rather than four discrete steps. Measured density spans 4.0 to 400.0 cells/m2, against 400.0 cells/m2 for the uniform baseline - 10.0x coarser in the far field, which is what the memory saving buys.

Adaptive vs uniform 5 cm - measured head to head

Every number here is measured on this frame by building a dense single-band uniform 5 cm lattice with the same projection engine the adaptive map uses, then timing projection and temporal fusion for both maps (median of 3 runs). It is the same frame, the same machine and the same code paths - nothing is modelled or extrapolated. The adaptive grid wins the axes that decide whether the map fits and keeps up: 25.0× fewer cells (502,400 vs 12,578,286), 17.6× faster per frame (60.9 ms vs 1070.2 ms, i.e. 16.4 vs 0.9 FPS) and 25.0× less resident memory. The dense lattice is slower because temporal fusion visits every allocated cell every frame - 12,578,286 cells - while the adaptive grid only carries 502,400. The one axis the uniform lattice wins is occupied-cell-only storage (0.4 MiB vs 1.1 MiB), because an empty cell is free to store - it is still paid for in latency above. That trade-off, spelled out rather than hidden, is the point of the panel.

Discrete objects - non-ground cells grouped into blobs

Occupied cells whose semantic class is not drivable are pooled onto a 0.5 m Cartesian lattice and merged with 8-connectivity, so a wall, a pole or a parked car becomes one row instead of hundreds of cells. Blobs are kept when they cover at least 5 cells, reach 0.3 m above the ground plane and sit within 100 m. Rows are ordered by distance from the sensor and the nearest are shown.
